Kataoka Kei on Dharmottara's Theory of Apoha: A Summary of His Published Papers (Japanese and English)

I have put together a single page that summarizes, with figures and tables, the claims Kataoka Kei has made in eight papers on the apoha theory of the eighth-century Buddhist logician Dharmottara. The page is available in Japanese and in English.

The central claim

For Dharmottara, the object of conceptual cognition and of words is neither a form within cognition nor an external real. It is something fabricated (āropita): presented as existing although it does not exist. On this one point, Kataoka argues, Dharmottara departs decisively from his master Dharmakīrti.

What the page covers

  • Three models of apoha (external, internal, neither internal nor external), and Kataoka's response to Frauwallner's assessment
  • āropita as "fabricated" rather than "superimposed"
  • Dharmottara's rejection of all four readings of "determination" (adhyavasāya)
  • Dharmakīrti himself as the real target of the criticism
  • Sameness imposed from above by a single fabricated object (a top-down model)
  • How Jayanta, Sucarita and Vācaspati distinguished two Buddhist theories of apoha
  • Reconstructing the lost Sanskrit, and Dharmottara's place in intellectual history
  • A table of the papers at a glance, with full references
